Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Los Angeles

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a system that runs for years and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of summer.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.

Common HVAC Problems in Los Angeles

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Long cooling seasons push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and salt air and moisture chew at outdoor condenser coils and fins, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Dirty filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, a slow kind of damage most homeowners never see coming.

We also see the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, refrigerant leaks that turn into long lukewarm afternoons, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much less expensive when caught early. If your system is doing anything unusual, strange noises, weak airflow, rooms that won't hold temperature, bills creeping upward, a quick diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes a costly breakdown.
